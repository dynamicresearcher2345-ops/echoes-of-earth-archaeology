Before written history

Prehistoric people, seen through the evidence

Archaeologists learn about early humans from fossils, stone tools, shelters, burials and traces of food and fire. These clues help reconstruct how people adapted to changing environments and lived in communities.

Waterways and civilization

Rivers & their histories

Rivers provided water, fertile land and routes for travel and trade. They shaped settlement, farming and the growth of cities.

≈

Indus

Harappan cities along the Indus and its tributaries developed planned streets, craft industries and long-distance trade during the Bronze Age.

≈

Nile

Seasonal flooding enriched Nile farmland. Ancient Egyptian communities depended on the river for agriculture, transport and connections between settlements.

≈

Tigris & Euphrates

Irrigation supported early Mesopotamian cities and states. Clay tablets preserve records of administration, trade, literature and law.

≈

Ganges

The Ganges basin became a major region for agriculture, settlement and trade, with enduring cultural and religious traditions.

A broad chronology

From stone tools to iron

These are broad labels, not one universal calendar. Dates and transitions varied by region.

MILLIONS OF YEARS AGO – c. 10,000 BCE*

Stone Age

Stone tools span Paleolithic and later periods. People hunted, gathered, made art and gradually developed farming in different places.

DATES VARY BY REGION

Copper Age

Also called the Chalcolithic. Some communities used copper alongside stone tools; copper working appeared at different times.

c. 3300 BCE ONWARD, REGIONALLY

Bronze Age

Bronze-making, expanding trade and urban societies emerged in several regions, including the Indus civilization and ancient Egypt.

c. 1200 BCE ONWARD, REGIONALLY

Iron Age

Iron technologies spread at different times. In South Asia, iron use is evidenced in the early first millennium BCE, with regional variation.

DATES VARY BY REGION

Historic periods

Writing, states and recorded histories developed at different times. Archaeology continues to add detail beyond written accounts.

*The Stone Age is a broad archaeological term; it does not begin or end at the same time everywhere.
