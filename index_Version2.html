Before written history

Prehistoric people, seen through the evidence

Archaeologists learn about early humans from fossils, stone tools, shelters, burials and traces of food and fire. These clues help reconstruct how people adapted to changing environments and lived in communities.

Waterways and civilization

Rivers & their histories

Rivers provided water, fertile land and routes for travel and trade. They shaped settlement, farming and the growth of cities.

≈

Indus

Along the Indus and its tributaries, Harappan cities such as Mohenjo-daro and Harappa developed planned streets, craft industries and long-distance trade during the Bronze Age.

≈

Nile

Seasonal flooding enriched the Nile valley’s farmland. Ancient Egyptian communities depended on the river for agriculture, transport and connections between settlements.

≈

Tigris & Euphrates

Between these rivers, irrigation supported early cities and states in Mesopotamia. Clay tablets preserve records of administration, trade, literature and law.

≈

Ganges

The Ganges basin became a major region for agriculture, settlement and trade. Its history includes ancient urban centers and enduring cultural and religious traditions.

A broad chronology

From stone tools to iron

These are broad labels, not one universal calendar. Dates and transitions varied by region.

MILLIONS OF YEARS AGO – c. 10,000 BCE*

Stone Age

Stone tools span the Paleolithic and later periods. People hunted, gathered, made art and gradually developed farming in different places.

DATES VARY BY REGION

Copper Age

Also called the Chalcolithic. Some communities used copper alongside stone tools; copper working appeared at different times in different regions.

c. 3300 BCE ONWARD, REGIONALLY

Bronze Age

Bronze-making, expanding trade and urban societies emerged in several regions. The Indus civilization and ancient Egypt flourished in this broad period.

c. 1200 BCE ONWARD, REGIONALLY

Iron Age

Iron tools and technologies spread at different times. In South Asia, iron use is evidenced in the early first millennium BCE, with regional variation.

DATES VARY BY REGION

Historic periods

Writing, states and recorded histories developed at different times. Archaeology continues to add detail beyond written accounts.

*The Stone Age is a broad archaeological term; it does not begin or end at the same time everywhere. Period labels are simplified for an introduction.
